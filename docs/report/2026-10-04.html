Where it stands

Every figure on this page is read out of the repository by tools/report-facts.sh rather than recalled. 582 tests pass, none fail, three are todo. tsc and oxlint are clean. The whole history is on main under one author; the eight claude/* branches are all zero ahead and exist only as stale pointers.

The project set out to make exercises that follow real patterns that exist, not random. That requirement is what the loop spent itself on, and it is the part that came out well. What it did not spend itself on is the microphone — which is the premise the whole app rests on, and which does not exist in any form.

What the loop actually bought

The musical assets are the result worth keeping: 37 rhythm cells, 26 named whole-bar patterns and 35 progression templates, each validated at construction and tested as data rather than one generated seed at a time. A wrong template is wrong in every key at once, so it is found once.

Two structural decisions did most of the work. Pitches are stored as a letter, an alteration and an octave rather than as MIDI numbers, which is why G♯ and A♭ stay distinct and why a C blues scale spells G♭ and G instead of F♯ and G. And roman numerals stay symbolic until the last moment, so the degree fixes the root's letter before any accidental is chosen — which is the whole reason V/V in C comes out D–F♯–A.

The generators are tested by breaking what the guards protect and watching the test go red. That repeatedly found guards that could not fail. It also found a real defect: a plagal close produced the exact V–IV retrogression the harmony generator exists to prevent, in 358 of 600 runs, and the existing repair could not fix it because both chords were locked by cadence writes.

Not built

No audio capture. The app cannot hear anything. The analysis chain that would process the sound is finished and tested off-device, and the seam it will plug into is decided (ADR 0012: judging consumes performed notes, never audio, so the same grader serves both a microphone and a MIDI keyboard). But nothing captures. Every exercise is answered by tapping a button, which is not the app that was asked for.

No melody generator. The largest single piece of the original design — contour planning, motivic development, and a beam search that makes every note record why it belongs — does not exist. Sight reading cannot be built until it does, and it is the part that decides whether generated music sounds like music or merely obeys the rules.

Neither shipping target. There is no service worker and no Android project. Both are pinned as todo tests in src/architecture.test.ts, so they fail the moment someone claims otherwise, but today the app runs in a dev server and nowhere else.

Open: the missing last note

On the Rhythms reference screen, the first press of a play button appears to drop the final note of the figure. It is reproducible for the person who reported it and has not been reproduced in a test.

Four explanations have been eliminated with evidence: the scheduling arithmetic was checked against every figure in every metre, cold-graph headroom was measured at 4.5 ms against 60 ms available with no note starting in the past, 92 envelopes were checked for malformation, and the master-gain stop window was ruled out. None of them accounts for it.

Progress now depends on one answer, which has not come back:

Does it happen on the first press after a page reload whatever the figure is, or on the first press of each particular figure?

The two point at different causes. The first implicates the audio graph waking up; the second implicates something cached per figure. Until it is known which, any fix is a guess.

Also open

The notation bundle is 377 kB gzipped and loads on the home screen. It is split into its own chunk, but the import is static, so the built index.html modulepreloads it: the split bought nothing on first load. Total JS is 465 kB gzipped against a planned 91 kB — the gap is the Bravura font, chosen deliberately, costed wrongly. Making the import dynamic would take 377 kB off the first paint for a reader who opens the home screen and stops there, and the architect has confirmed it is compatible with ADR 0003 — exactly one runtime import reaches the renderer, because the one-importer rule held. But it must not be done first: a lazy chunk that the service worker does not precache breaks the app offline, and vite-plugin-pwa is still a declared dependency that vite.config.ts never imports. Wire the PWA, then split.

Three progression templates are unreachable on the default settings path, and leading-tone-close is one of them. The decision was deferred to when the progression exercise is built, which is now the next piece of work rather than a distant one.

Four documents say the DSP layer computes chroma. It does not. Corrected after this report was first published, by the architect, who wrote the worst of them. src/audio/dsp/ exports FFT utilities, an onset detector, YIN pitch detection and rhythm alignment; the string chroma appears nowhere under src/audio/. ADR 0013 and ADR 0012 both say a chord’s pitch classes are “answerable from the chroma the DSP layer already computes”, ADR 0001 lists it among that directory’s contents, and so does the diagram in docs/adr/README.md. The source of all four is one line of CLAUDE.md describing what the layer is for, read by each subsequent document as a description of what it holds.

This matters beyond tidiness, and it changes the order of work. The measurement ADR 0013 wanted cannot be run at all, because there is nothing to measure. The chord exercise cannot be built on that claim. The sequence is: build a chroma feature, measure per-pitch-class recall on recorded chords both strummed and blocked, then build the exercise on whichever answer comes back — and if recall on inner voices is poor, the chord exercise declares itself MIDI-first, which ADR 0012 already permits an exercise to do.

It is also the second time: ADR 0005 corrected ADR 0002 for the same mistake, a claim about this codebase taken from the prose around it rather than from the code. Worth recording plainly rather than filing quietly, because the lesson did not take.

Next

  1. Capture, end to end, on one exercise. getUserMedia with echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control all off, a worklet, and a note assembler feeding the seam ADR 0012 already specifies. Rhythm is the exercise to prove it on: the onset detector and the aligner are both finished, so it tests the plumbing and not the algorithms.
  2. The three exercises that need no new machinery — progression ID, scale ID, absolute note ID. This is the cheapest way to close the gap between the home screen and the brief, and it settles the leading-tone-close question as a side effect.
  3. The melody generator, built motif-first rather than bolted onto a legal-but-dull walk. Its real failure mode is blandness, which no test catches, so it needs listening as well as invariants.
  4. Sight reading, which is the first exercise that uses everything at once.
  5. The two shipping targets, taking the two todo tests green. The service worker moves ahead of the bundle split for the reason above, and the chroma measurement sits in front of the chord exercise.

Answering the Rhythms question is not on that list because it costs one sentence rather than a work item, but it blocks the only user-reported defect currently open.
